A giant pink Censor Eraser has folded the Hare into a paper airplane to "rewrite" the show into something boring. This time the Wolf is not chasing his rival to eat him. He is chasing the Eraser to get him back.

The story

Wolf is mid-chase when the sky tears like wet paper. The Censor Eraser wipes the color from Moscow and kidnaps the Hare, flying off toward the Soyuzmultfilm Studio Tower. Wolf, a flat, foldable hooligan armed with a guitar, must team up with old rivals to follow.

Wolf glides like a paper plane, rolls up into a tube, and brings a partner for every problem. Four parts, one hour, one very determined Wolf.

Four parts

Prologue

The Torn Cel

A painted Moscow street. Meet Hedgehog, your spiky shield and projectile, and learn timed jumps and guitar smashes on two Ink Blots.

Chapter 1

The Cardboard Beach

Glide across the missing bridge, pull the giant paper fastener to unfold a dock, recruit the Lifeguard, and beat the Mechanical Cat Magician and his hat.

Chapter 2

The Origami Construction Site

Roll up under low steel beams, dodge wrecking balls, play a guitar solo for a family of picnicking pigs, and meet Fox, whose tail reveals invisible ink.

Finale

The Animator's Desk

Colors fade to pencil sketches as you face the Robot Hare, folded into an armored origami mech and supercharged by the Eraser.

Three partners, three ways to fight

Hedgehog

Spike Roll in the field, and he counters any attacker you Guard.

Lifeguard Hippo

Belly Flop smashes heavy crates, and his weight tips the Magician's hat. Hits extra hard.

Fox

Her tail sweeps away invisible ink to reveal hidden platforms, and Ink Flash exposes enemy weak points.

Timed action commands

Land your jumps on the beat, wind up the guitar smash in the green zone, and press at the last instant to Guard or Super Guard.

Applause on a real stage

A live audience of forest animals cheers your good moves. Fill the Applause meter for a team combo.

Paper tricks

Glide across gaps, roll up under beams, and use partner abilities to open the path.
